A probability in either representation

The same prepared state |ψ⟩ has position amplitudes ψ(x) = ⟨x|ψ⟩ and momentum amplitudes φ(p) = ⟨p|ψ⟩. A narrow position bin has probability approximately |ψ(x)|² Δx, where Δx here is the bin width and the density varies little across it. Sum over bins and take the limit to get an integral. The momentum calculation uses the same reasoning.

P(a ≤ X ≤ b) = ∫ab |ψ(x)|² dx

P(c ≤ P ≤ d) = ∫cd |φ(p)|² dp

∫−∞∞ |ψ(x)|² dx = ∫−∞∞ |φ(p)|² dp = 1

Here X and P denote the random outcomes of position and momentum measurements. The integration variables x and p specify coordinates. An exact point has probability zero for these continuous distributions. Position density has units nm⁻¹, and momentum density has units (ℏ/nm)⁻¹.

Each momentum p corresponds to a spatial phase pattern exp(ipx/ℏ). The Fourier transform computes the amplitude of that pattern in ψ. With ℏ the reduced Planck constant, px/ℏ is dimensionless. In the lab’s numerical units, x is entered in nm and p in ℏ/nm, so their numerical product gives that ratio.

φ(p) = (2πℏ)−1/2 ∫−∞∞ ψ(x) exp(−ipx/ℏ) dx

ψ(x) = (2πℏ)−1/2 ∫−∞∞ φ(p) exp(+ipx/ℏ) dp

These transforms preserve the norm. Switching views changes which representation you inspect. Run a detection to simulate a measurement on a new preparation.

Shift a peak or change its spread

For a Gaussian centered at x = 0, let σ be its position standard deviation and p₀ its central momentum. The factor exp(ip₀x/ℏ) has magnitude one, so changing p₀ keeps the entire position density fixed.

ψ(x) = (2πσ²)−1/4 exp[−x²/(4σ²)] exp(ip₀x/ℏ)

In the Fourier integral, exp(ip₀x/ℏ) exp(−ipx/ℏ) = exp[−i(p−p₀)x/ℏ]. Write q = p−p₀. The Gaussian integral of exp[−x²/(4σ²)] exp(−iqx/ℏ) is 2σ√π exp[−σ²q²/ℏ²]. Thus the momentum amplitude is proportional to exp[−σ²(p−p₀)²/ℏ²]. Its squared magnitude is the normalized density

|φ(p)|² = exp[−(p−p₀)²/(2σp²)]/(√(2π)σp)

σp = ℏ/(2σ),   ⟨P⟩ = p₀

Now use Δx for the standard deviation of X. By definition, Δx = √⟨(X−⟨X⟩)²⟩ and Δp = √⟨(P−⟨P⟩)²⟩. The uncertainty bound from the earlier lesson is ΔxΔp ≥ |⟨[X,P]⟩|/2. The position and momentum operators obey [X,P] = iℏ, so the lower bound is ℏ/2. For this unchirped Gaussian, Δx = σ and Δp = ℏ/(2σ), which attain that bound. These widths describe the spread of outcomes across fresh preparations. Detector resolution and the uncertainty of a fitted mean describe different quantities.

Worked example · one standard deviation in either view

At σ = 1 nm and p₀ = 0, the momentum width is 0.5 ℏ/nm. The position gate [−1,+1] nm and the momentum gate [−0.5,+0.5] ℏ/nm each cover one standard deviation on either side of their center. Each therefore contains about 68.27%.

Halving σ to 0.5 nm doubles Δp to 1 ℏ/nm. Changing only p₀ translates the momentum curve and keeps both widths fixed. This answers the opening question. Even when two sources have identical position densities, a momentum detector can see a difference in their spatial phases.

Two packets give a momentum interference pattern

Let gL and gR be real Gaussian amplitudes of individual width σ centered at −d/2 and +d/2. Put phase θ on the right packet and apply the same carrier phase exp(ip₀x/ℏ) to the entire state.

ψ(x) = exp(ip₀x/ℏ)[gL(x) + exp(iθ)gR(x)]/√D

s = ∫−∞∞ gLgR dx = exp[−d²/(8σ²)]

D = 1 + 1 + 2s cos θ = 2(1+s cos θ)

The first two normalization terms are the individual packet norms. The cross term integrates to 2s cos θ because the packets overlap. The common carrier cancels from the position density.

Let G(q) be the normalized momentum amplitude for a single unshifted Gaussian, with q = p−p₀. Translating a packet by x₀ gives a Fourier phase exp(−iqx₀/ℏ) in this carrier-shifted transform. The left and right packet translations therefore give opposite signs in the exponent.

φ(p) = G(q)[exp(+iqd/(2ℏ)) + exp(iθ) exp(−iqd/(2ℏ))]/√D

|φ(p)|² = |G(q)|² [1 + cos(θ−qd/ℏ)]/(1+s cos θ)

In Momentum Space, compare 0°, 90°, and 180°. The 0° pattern has constructive interference at p₀. At 180° the two contributions cancel there, giving a node. A finite gate around the node can still collect probability. Intermediate phases can make the density asymmetric, so the actual mean may differ from the carrier p₀.

Calculate the combined widths

Define Z = 1+s cos θ, κ = d/ℏ, and t = ℏ/(2σ), the momentum width of one component. The position density is even, so ⟨X⟩ = 0. Its second moment includes the two offsets and the overlap term.

Δx² = σ² + d²/(4Z)

⟨q⟩ = sκt² sin θ/Z,   ⟨P⟩ = p₀ + ⟨q⟩

⟨q²⟩ = t² − sκ²t⁴ cos θ/Z

Δp² = ⟨q²⟩ − ⟨q⟩²

The momentum terms follow by differentiating the Fourier integral of the Gaussian density |G(q)|², whose cosine average at κ is exp(−κ²t²/2) = s. The lab computes the variance about the actual mean. Gaussian gate integrals use the normal cumulative distribution. Integrating the oscillatory momentum density uses numerical quadrature on the chosen interval, independent of the displayed graph.
