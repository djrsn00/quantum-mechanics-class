Lesson 17

Time Evolution

In the Stern–Gerlach lesson, passing only the positive X channel prepared the spin in |+x⟩. An immediate X measurement would give + with certainty. Now let the prepared spin spend time in a uniform, constant magnetic field along Z before measuring it.

How long must we wait before an X measurement is certain to give −, and what would a Z measurement show at that same instant?

A fixed Hamiltonian changes the state

The time-dependent Schrödinger equation gives the rate of change of the ket |ψ(t)⟩, the quantum state at elapsed time t in seconds.

iℏ d|ψ(t)⟩/dt = H|ψ(t)⟩

Here i is the imaginary unit, ℏ is the reduced Planck constant in joule-seconds, and H is the Hamiltonian. It is the energy operator, with eigenvalues measured in joules, and determines how the state changes. For time-independent H, the evolution operator U(t) is a matrix exponential, denoted by exp.

U(t) = exp(−iHt/ℏ)

|ψ(t)⟩ = U(t)|ψ(0)⟩

The Hamiltonian is Hermitian, so U(t) is unitary. It preserves the norm of the state. A normalized state therefore keeps total probability 1 in every complete measurement basis.

Energy levels in the Z basis

Use the ordered basis (|+z⟩, |−z⟩). The Pauli operator σz has eigenvalue +1 on |+z⟩ and −1 on |−z⟩. The model takes the angular frequency ω to be positive, in radians per second.

H = (ℏω/2)σz

E+ = +ℏω/2,   E− = −ℏω/2

ΔE = E+ − E− = ℏω

ω = ΔE/ℏ,   T = 2π/ω

E+ and E− are the energies of the two Z states. This sign convention puts |+z⟩ higher in energy and fixes the positive right-hand sense of rotation about +z. The energy gap ΔE sets ω. For this lab ω = 2π rad/s, so the precession period T is 1 s.

From phase to probabilities

  1. Evolve each energy component

    On an energy eigenstate with energy E, the Hamiltonian acts by multiplication by E. The exponential U(t) therefore gives that component the factor exp(−iEt/ℏ). Begin with |+x⟩ = (|+z⟩ + |−z⟩)/√2, and write θ = ωt for the angle accumulated in radians. Using the two energy eigenvalues gives the Z-basis amplitudes a and b.

    |ψ(t)⟩ = a|+z⟩ + b|−z⟩

    a = exp(−iθ/2)/√2

    b = exp(+iθ/2)/√2

  2. Identify the relative phase

    The notation arg gives the phase angle of a nonzero complex amplitude. Here arg(b) − arg(a) = +θ modulo 2π. Factoring exp(−iθ/2) out of the full ket gives this physically equivalent representative.

    (|+z⟩ + exp(iθ)|−z⟩)/√2

    A common phase multiplies every projected amplitude by the same factor of magnitude 1. It cancels when the Born rule takes the squared magnitude. The lab’s a and b readouts keep that factor so you can see the full evolution.

  3. Project onto measurement outcomes

    Projecting onto |+x⟩ and |−x⟩ gives (a+b)/√2 and (a−b)/√2, respectively. Substituting the evolved amplitudes reduces these to cos(θ/2) and −i sin(θ/2). The Born rule gives the probabilities P by squaring the magnitudes.

    P(+x) = (1 + cos θ)/2

    P(−x) = (1 − cos θ)/2

    P(+z) = |a|² = 1/2

    P(−z) = |b|² = 1/2

    The X projections combine the two energy components, so the relative phase changes how they interfere. The Z projections each pick out a single component whose magnitude is fixed. On the Bloch sphere, the vector (cos θ, sin θ, 0) moves from +x toward +y about +z, and its x component determines the difference P(+x) − P(−x).

Worked example · the first certain X−

The first angle with cos θ = −1 is θ = π. This occurs at t = π/ω = T/2 = 0.5 s. In the factored representative, exp(iπ) = −1, so the spin is in the physical state |−x⟩. The X measurement gives − with 100% probability. A Z measurement on a spin with the same preparation and wait still gives 50% for each outcome.

At T = 1 s, the relative phase is 0 modulo 2π and the Bloch vector and probabilities return to their opening values. The full ket is −|+x⟩ because exp(−iπ) = exp(+iπ) = −1. The minus sign is a global phase.

Compare an energy eigenstate

Starting instead from |+z⟩ gives a = exp(−iθ/2) and b = 0. The relative phase is undefined because a zero amplitude has no phase. The Bloch vector stays at +z. The Z probabilities remain 1 and 0, and the X probabilities remain 1/2 and 1/2.

A single energy eigenstate acquires only a global phase, so its probabilities stay constant in any fixed measurement basis. A superposition of different energies can show changing probabilities in a basis whose projections combine those components.
