Lesson 17

Time Evolution

In the Stern–Gerlach lesson, passing only the positive X channel prepared the spin in |+x⟩. An immediate X measurement would give + with certainty. Now let the prepared spin spend time in a uniform, constant magnetic field along Z before measuring it.

How long must we wait before an X measurement is certain to give −, and what would a Z measurement show at that same instant?

Open the optional Time Evolution Arcade

A fixed Hamiltonian changes the state

The time-dependent Schrödinger equation gives the rate of change of the ket |ψ(t)⟩, the quantum state at elapsed time t in seconds.

iℏ d|ψ(t)⟩/dt = H|ψ(t)⟩

Here i is the imaginary unit, ℏ is the reduced Planck constant in joule-seconds, and H is the Hamiltonian. It is the energy operator, with eigenvalues measured in joules, and determines how the state changes. For time-independent H, the evolution operator U(t) is a matrix exponential, denoted by exp.

U(t) = exp(−iHt/ℏ)

|ψ(t)⟩ = U(t)|ψ(0)⟩

The Hamiltonian is Hermitian, so U(t) is unitary. It preserves the norm of the state. A normalized state therefore keeps total probability 1 in every complete measurement basis.

Energy levels in the Z basis

Use the ordered basis (|+z⟩, |−z⟩). The Pauli operator σz has eigenvalue +1 on |+z⟩ and −1 on |−z⟩. The model takes the angular frequency ω to be positive, in radians per second.

H = (ℏω/2)σz

E+ = +ℏω/2,   E− = −ℏω/2

ΔE = E+ − E− = ℏω

ω = ΔE/ℏ,   T = 2π/ω

E+ and E− are the energies of the two Z states. This sign convention puts |+z⟩ higher in energy and fixes the positive right-hand sense of rotation about +z. The energy gap ΔE sets ω. For this lab ω = 2π rad/s, so the precession period T is 1 s.

From phase to probabilities

  1. Evolve each energy component

    On an energy eigenstate with energy E, the Hamiltonian acts by multiplication by E. The exponential U(t) therefore gives that component the factor exp(−iEt/ℏ). Begin with |+x⟩ = (|+z⟩ + |−z⟩)/√2, and write θ = ωt for the angle accumulated in radians. Using the two energy eigenvalues gives the Z-basis amplitudes a and b.

    |ψ(t)⟩ = a|+z⟩ + b|−z⟩

    a = exp(−iθ/2)/√2

    b = exp(+iθ/2)/√2

  2. Identify the relative phase

    The notation arg gives the phase angle of a nonzero complex amplitude. Here arg(b) − arg(a) = +θ modulo 2π. Factoring exp(−iθ/2) out of the full ket gives this physically equivalent representative.

    (|+z⟩ + exp(iθ)|−z⟩)/√2

    A common phase multiplies every projected amplitude by the same factor of magnitude 1. It cancels when the Born rule takes the squared magnitude. The lab’s a and b readouts keep that factor so you can see the full evolution.

  3. Project onto measurement outcomes

    Projecting onto |+x⟩ and |−x⟩ gives (a+b)/√2 and (a−b)/√2, respectively. Substituting the evolved amplitudes reduces these to cos(θ/2) and −i sin(θ/2). The Born rule gives the probabilities P by squaring the magnitudes.

    P(+x) = (1 + cos θ)/2

    P(−x) = (1 − cos θ)/2

    P(+z) = |a|² = 1/2

    P(−z) = |b|² = 1/2

    The X projections combine the two energy components, so the relative phase changes how they interfere. The Z projections each pick out a single component whose magnitude is fixed. On the Bloch sphere, the vector (cos θ, sin θ, 0) moves from +x toward +y about +z, and its x component determines the difference P(+x) − P(−x).

Worked example · the first certain X−

The first angle with cos θ = −1 is θ = π. This occurs at t = π/ω = T/2 = 0.5 s. In the factored representative, exp(iπ) = −1, so the spin is in the physical state |−x⟩. The X measurement gives − with 100% probability. A Z measurement on a spin with the same preparation and wait still gives 50% for each outcome.

At T = 1 s, the relative phase is 0 modulo 2π and the Bloch vector and probabilities return to their opening values. The full ket is −|+x⟩ because exp(−iπ) = exp(+iπ) = −1. The minus sign is a global phase.

Compare an energy eigenstate

Starting instead from |+z⟩ gives a = exp(−iθ/2) and b = 0. The relative phase is undefined because a zero amplitude has no phase. The Bloch vector stays at +z. The Z probabilities remain 1 and 0, and the X probabilities remain 1/2 and 1/2.

A single energy eigenstate acquires only a global phase, so its probabilities stay constant in any fixed measurement basis. A superposition of different energies can show changing probabilities in a basis whose projections combine those components.

Energy Ladder

The energies of a system are the eigenvalues of its Hamiltonian H. For a 2×2 H they are E₊ ≥ E₋, the gap is ΔE = E₊ − E₋, and the gap sets the angular frequency ω = ΔE/ℏ and the period T = 2π/ω = h/ΔE. Enter H in µeV (10⁻⁶ eV). H is Hermitian, so its diagonal is real and the entry in row 2, column 1 is the conjugate of the one in row 1, column 2. The ladder shows the diagonal entries dashed and the energies solid.

Show the math

E± = (H₁₁ + H₂₂)/2 ± √[((H₁₁ − H₂₂)/2)² + |H₁₂|²], so ΔE = 2√[((H₁₁ − H₂₂)/2)² + |H₁₂|²]. Mixing (H₁₂ ≠ 0) always pushes the levels apart.

ω = ΔE/ℏ in rad/s, f = ΔE/h = ω/2π in Hz and T = 1/f. With ℏ = 6.582119569 × 10⁻¹⁶ eV·s, 1 µeV corresponds to ω ≈ 1.519 × 10⁹ rad/s and f ≈ 241.8 MHz.

Phase Clocks

Each energy eigenstate is a clock. Under H, the amplitude of an eigenstate with energy E is multiplied by exp(−iEt/ℏ): its hand turns clockwise at the rate E/ℏ. Here a is the amplitude of |+z⟩ with energy E₊, and b the amplitude of |−z⟩ with energy E₋, so H = diag(E₊, E₋). Change the rates, add a common shift V to both energies, and watch which readouts move. Play respects your reduced-motion setting and the Reduced effects box; Step always works.

Show the math

Shifting H → H + V I multiplies the whole ket by exp(−iVt/ℏ), a global phase. So the zero of energy is a free choice, and only energy differences can be seen.

Field Dial (3D)

Point a uniform field B in any direction n and the Hamiltonian is H = (ℏω/2) n·σ. The Bloch vector precesses right-handedly about n at the angular frequency ω, completing one turn per period T, and it keeps its angle to the field. Set the field and the start with the dials, or focus the sphere and use the arrow keys to turn the field. Drag the sphere or use the view sliders to turn the view. Positive rotation is right-handed: with your right thumb along the field, your fingers curl the way the vector moves. This matches the lab, where a field along +z takes +x toward +y.

Show the math

For H = (ℏω/2) n·σ, U(t) = cos(θ/2) I − i sin(θ/2) n·σ with θ = ωt. On the Bloch sphere this is a rotation by θ about n.

Rodrigues’ formula gives the turned vector: v(θ) = v cos θ + (n × v) sin θ + n(n·v)(1 − cos θ). The component along n never changes, so the vector stays on a cone around the field.

Pulse Workshop

A strong static field along z sets the spin’s energy levels. A short extra field B₁ across it, along a direction φ in the xy-plane, acts as a pulse. In the frame turning with the spin, the pulse simply turns the state about its own axis at a fixed rate, here ω = π/2 rad/µs (T = 4 µs). The angle turned, θ = ωt, is the pulse area: t = 2 µs gives a π pulse (a full flip) and t = 1 µs a π/2 pulse (a half flip). Build a sequence of up to three pulses; each starts from |+z⟩.

In MRI, a π/2 pulse tips the protons’ magnetization into the transverse plane, where its precession induces the signal the scanner records; a π pulse inverts it. In quantum computing, a π pulse about x is the X (NOT) gate, and π/2 pulses build superpositions. Real pulses work in a frame rotating with the spin’s precession; this station works in that frame.

    Error Detective controls

    Case 1 · from gap to frequency✓ Complete

    Two levels are ΔE = 4 µeV apart. How long is one period of precession?

    1. ΔE = 4 × 10⁻⁶ eV × 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J/eV = 6.409 × 10⁻²⁵ J.
    2. ω = ΔE = 6.409 × 10⁻²⁵ rad/s.
    3. T = 2π/ω ≈ 9.80 × 10²⁴ s.
    4. So the spin takes longer than the age of the universe to turn once.
    Case 2 · which way it turns✓ Complete

    H = (ℏω/2)σx and |ψ(0)⟩ = |+z⟩ = (|+x⟩ + |−x⟩)/√2. Where is the Bloch vector at θ = ωt = π/2?

    1. The energies are +ℏω/2 on |+x⟩ and −ℏω/2 on |−x⟩.
    2. Each component gains exp(+iEt/ℏ): |ψ(t)⟩ = (e^(+iθ/2)|+x⟩ + e^(−iθ/2)|−x⟩)/√2.
    3. In the Z basis this is cos(θ/2)|+z⟩ + i sin(θ/2)|−z⟩, which at θ = π/2 is |+y⟩.
    4. So the spin turns from +z toward +y.
    Case 3 · one full period✓ Complete

    H = (ℏω/2)σy and |ψ(0)⟩ = |+z⟩. What does a Z measurement give after one period T?

    1. After one period, θ = ωT = 2π.
    2. U(T) = cos(π) I − i sin(π) σy = −I.
    3. So |ψ(T)⟩ = −|+z⟩.
    4. The minus sign has reversed the spin, so a Z measurement now gives − with certainty.
    Case 4 · a quarter period✓ Complete

    H = (ℏω/2)σx and |ψ(0)⟩ = |+z⟩. Find P(+z) at t = T/4.

    1. At t = T/4, θ = ωt = π/2.
    2. The amplitude of |+z⟩ is cos θ = cos(π/2) = 0.
    3. So P(+z) = 0.
    4. The spin has reached −z after a quarter period.
    Case 5 · an energy eigenstate✓ Complete

    H = (ℏω/2)σy and |ψ(0)⟩ = |+y⟩. Describe |ψ(t)⟩.

    1. |+y⟩ is an eigenstate of H with energy E = +ℏω/2.
    2. So |ψ(t)⟩ = |+y⟩ for every t: the state does not evolve at all.
    3. Its probabilities in every basis stay constant.
    4. So adding a constant V to H changes nothing about |ψ(t)⟩.
    Show the math

    The planted mistakes: ω = ΔE/ℏ, never ΔE itself. Each energy component gains exp(−iEt/ℏ), with a minus sign. A global factor such as the −1 at T changes no probability. U(t) contains cos(θ/2) and sin(θ/2), half the Bloch sphere angle θ = ωt. An energy eigenstate does evolve, but only by a global phase.

    In the frame turning at the average rate, a spin with offset δ has phase φ = δt. A π pulse about x at τ maps φ to −φ, after which φ = −δτ + δ(t − τ) = δ(t − 2τ). Every φ is 0 at t = 2τ, whatever δ is.

    |M| is the length of the average of the unit arrows (cos φ, sin φ). For two spins at ±δ, |M| = |cos δt|.

    Real-World Precession

    A spin in a field B precesses at ω = γB, where γ, the gyromagnetic ratio, belongs to the particle. For a proton γ/2π = 42.577 MHz per tesla; for a free electron |γ|/2π = 28.025 GHz per tesla. The same two-level physics sets the tick of an atomic clock, where the gap ΔE = hf between two hyperfine levels defines the second. Answers that use these constants count within 0.5%.
