Mathematical console

Potential, Hamiltonian, and fixed units

V(x) = ½mω²x²
Ĥ = −(ℏ²/2m)d²/dx² + ½mω²x²

The first term gives kinetic energy and the second gives the bowl-shaped potential. The force is −dV/dx = −mω²x. Near a smooth stable minimum, the linear term in a Taylor expansion vanishes. If the curvature is positive, the quadratic term gives the leading restoring force.

Use fixed references mref and ωref. Then Lref = √[ℏ/(mrefωref)], Eref = ℏωref, tref = 1/ωref, and the momentum unit is ℏ/Lref. The local oscillator length ℓ = √[ℏ/(mω)] sets ξ = x/ℓ. The period is T = 2π/ω and ordinary frequency is f = ω/(2π).

At fixed ω, a larger mass narrows the state and raises the potential curvature while preserving the energy levels. At fixed mass, a larger ω narrows the state, shortens the period, and widens the energy gaps. In a separate fixed-spring-constant experiment, ω = √(k/m), so varying m would also vary ω.

Show Me Why

1. Derive the ground state and the energy ladder

In ξ = x/ℓ, divide Ĥ by ℏω to get ½[−d²/dξ² + ξ²]. Define the lowering and raising operators

a = (ξ + d/dξ)/√2   a† = (ξ − d/dξ)/√2

By the product rule, (d/dξ)(ξu) = u + ξu′. This gives [a,a†] = 1 and a†a = ½[−d²/dξ² + ξ² − 1]. Hence Ĥ = ℏω(a†a + ½). The expectation of a†a is ‖a u‖², so ℏω/2 is a lower bound.

At the ground state, a u₀ = 0 gives u₀′(ξ) = −ξu₀(ξ), whose normalized solution is u₀(x) = e−ξ²/2/(π1/4√ℓ). It attains the lower bound.

The commutators [Ĥ,a†] = ℏωa† and [Ĥ,a] = −ℏωa shift a nonzero result by one rung. Repeated lowering must end at the ground state because the spectrum is bounded below. Normalization gives a†|n⟩ = √(n+1)|n+1⟩ and a|n⟩ = √n|n−1⟩.

Eₙ = ℏω(n + ½)   ΔE = ℏω

2. Read the signed wavefunction and its nodes

uₙ(x) = Hₙ(ξ)e−ξ²/2 / [π1/4√ℓ √(2ⁿn!)]

The physicists’ Hermite polynomials start with H₀ = 1 and H₁ = 2ξ. The recurrence is Hₙ₊₁ = 2ξHₙ − 2nHₙ₋₁. The Gaussian factor is nonzero at every finite x. The n real roots of Hₙ give the nodes, and multiplying each root by ℓ gives its physical position.

For n = 1 the node is x = 0. For n = 2, H₂ = 4ξ² − 2 puts the nodes at ±ℓ/√2. During time evolution the real trace can pass through zero while the imaginary part remains nonzero. Local phase is undefined at a zero of the full amplitude.

3. Work out the opening detector probabilities

Solving V(x) = Eₙ gives x = ±ℓ√(2n+1). A classical trajectory with that energy reaches those points with zero speed. In the quantum ground state the density is ρ₀(x) = e−x²/ℓ²/(√πℓ), with standard deviation Δx₀ = ℓ/√2.

P(a ≤ X ≤ b) = ∫ab |Ψ(x,t)|² dx
P(|X| > ℓ) = (2/√π)∫1∞ e−ξ² dξ = erfc(1) ≈ 15.73%

At the opening parameters ℓ = Lref, the turning points are ±Lref, the ground-state energy is 0.5 Eref, and Δx₀ ≈ 0.7071 Lref. A batch of 100 has an expected count of about 16 beyond the turning points, with variation between batches.

For Ψ = uₙe−iEₙt/ℏ, the phase cancels in the squared magnitude. The stationary probability current is zero because uₙ can be chosen real and the phase is spatially uniform. A detection in a tail is compatible with stationary confinement.

4. Follow two phases and their interference

Ψ = cos α · uᵣe−iEᵣt/ℏ + sin α · uₛeiφ−iEₛt/ℏ

Orthonormality makes the total norm cos²α + sin²α = 1. The density includes a cross term 2 cos α sin α · uᵣuₛ cos δ(t), with δ(t) = φ − (Eₛ − Eᵣ)t/ℏ. When both weights are nonzero, the beat period is 2π/(|s − r|ω).

With r = 0, s = 1, α = 45°, and φ = 0, the cross term increases the right-half probability at t = 0 to ½ + 1/√(2π) ≈ 89.89%. At T/4 it gives 50.00%. At T/2 the right-half probability is about 10.11%.

5. Compare a coherent packet with a classical oscillator

q(t) = x₀ cos(ωt) + [p₀/(mω)] sin(ωt)
p(t) = p₀ cos(ωt) − mωx₀ sin(ωt)
ρ(x,t) = e−(x−q(t))²/ℓ²/(√πℓ)

The Gaussian keeps spread ℓ/√2. Its mean q(t) follows the classical solution with the same initial position and momentum. Individual position results are distributed around that mean.

The lab uses the displaced ground-state envelope times ei[p(t)(x−q(t)/2)/ℏ − ωt/2] to obtain the signed real and imaginary components. This analytic model includes the full coherent state, which generally uses infinitely many rungs. Its mean energy is ½ℏω + p₀²/(2m) + ½mω²x₀².

Revisit the opening. The stationary ground state gives a spread of detector positions without moving its mean. A coherent Gaussian can keep that same spread while its mean oscillates.
