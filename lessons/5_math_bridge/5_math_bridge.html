Why the two right triangles give the length

Start with the right triangle in the xy plane. Its perpendicular legs have lengths |x| and |y|. The diagonal from the origin to Pxy is rxy. In depth view, look for the second triangle. Its legs are that diagonal and the perpendicular depth |z|.

rxy2 = x2 + y2
r2 = rxy2 + z2

At (3, 4, 0), the first sum is 9 + 16 = 25 square units and depth adds zero. Taking the nonnegative square root gives 5 units of length. At (3, 4, 2), the second sum is 25 + 4 = 29 square units, so the length is √29 units, about 5.39.

Coordinates can have either sign because they describe directions from the origin. Squaring removes those signs. At (0, 0, 0), the length is zero and the arrow has no direction.

Angles, projections, and radians

In radius mode, the endpoint stays in the xy plane. Start on the positive x-axis and turn counterclockwise through angle θ. The angle arc shows that turn. The two projections give the signed horizontal and vertical components.

x = r cos θ
y = r sin θ

A radian measures angle as arc length divided by radius. On a circle of radius 5 units, a 5-unit arc spans one radian. A full turn covers arc length 2πr, so 360° equals 2π radians. Multiply degrees by π/180 to convert them to radians.

At 120° the tip is in the second quadrant. Here cos θ = −1/2 and sin θ = √3/2. The horizontal component is negative and the vertical component is positive. The length stays 5 because cos² θ + sin² θ = 1.

Where these tools show up next

Angles help describe phase when we introduce complex numbers. Arrows are our first example of vectors, and inner products give us a way to compare vectors. Bases let us describe the same vector with different coordinates. Matrices describe transformations, and eigenvectors point in directions those transformations preserve up to a scale factor. Calculus gives us tools for describing how a graph changes.

Keep the opening question in mind: the tip can move while the length stays fixed. Later we’ll use the same habit of asking what changes and what stays fixed when we work with quantum states.
