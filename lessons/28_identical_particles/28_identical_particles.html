Exchange and joint measurements

Repeated measurements in the same preparation sample a joint distribution. If the complete state has exchange sign −1, its value equals its own negative wherever both complete coordinates coincide, so the amplitude there is zero. If the two spin coordinates differ, equal spatial coordinates alone do not force the total amplitude to vanish.

Follow the coincidence amplitudes

a† → (c† + i d†)/√2
b† → (i c† + d†)/√2

  1. One detection at each output can come from both transmissions with amplitude +½ or both reflections with amplitude i²/2 = −½.
  2. For matched bosons the creation operators commute. Both alternatives produce the same state c†d†|0⟩ and their amplitudes sum to zero. The remaining normalized pair state is i(|2,0⟩ + |0,2⟩)/√2.
  3. With orthogonal markers the alternatives leave distinguishable states. Add ¼ and ¼ to obtain the coincidence probability ½.
  4. For matched, spin-polarized fermions, d†c† = −c†d†. Reordering the reflected term adds a second minus sign, so the coincidence amplitude is 1. The same-mode creation terms vanish. The orthogonal input ports allow the initial pair. Other spin preparations can give different spatial interference.
Temporal overlap M

Overlap measures how well the arrival modes match. Both packets have the same carrier and normalized amplitude envelope proportional to exp[−(t−t₀)²/(4σₜ²)]. Here σₜ is the standard deviation of the temporal intensity. With τ = tb−ta, the squared overlap is M = |⟨φa|φb⟩|² = exp[−τ²/(4σₜ²)]. Finite separation leaves a nonzero Gaussian overlap. Delay changes this overlap while the particle species stays fixed.

For these preparations, P(1,1) is (1−M)/2 for bosons, ½ for persistent orthogonal tags, and (1+M)/2 for spin-polarized fermions. Symmetry gives P(2,0) = P(0,2) = [1−P(1,1)]/2. Thus E[nc] = E[nd] = 1 for every supported setup.

At τ = 2σₜ√ln 2, M = ½, and the three coincidence probabilities are ¼, ½, and ¾. Independent trials fluctuate around those values. Try the splitter

Normalize the two exchange terms

A = a(x₁)b(x₂), B = b(x₁)a(x₂)
‖A ± B‖² = 1 + 1 ± (⟨A|B⟩ + ⟨B|A⟩) = 2 ± 2|s|²

The two unit norms come from the normalized modes. With s = ⟨a|b⟩, each cross term equals s·s* = |s|². For the Gaussian spatial modes s = exp[−d²/(8σₓ²)]. This overlap changes the norm of the constructed state. Temporal overlap at the splitter instead governs interference between alternatives originating in orthogonal ports.

+ and −

The plus sign adds A and B, leaving the sum invariant under exchange. The minus sign subtracts B from A, producing a sign reversal under exchange. Choose a combination

Normalization N

N₊ or N₋ scales A ± B to unit norm. N± = 1/√[2(1±|s|²)] when the denominator is nonzero. At a = b, the symmetric combination reduces to the product a(x₁)a(x₂). The antisymmetric numerator vanishes. Construct the states

Pauli exclusion and spin

Two electrons in the same complete mode produce identical product terms, and their difference is zero. With one electron in 1s↑ and one in 1s↓, the normalized antisymmetrized state factors into a common 1s(x₁)1s(x₂) spatial product and the spin singlet. For separable examples the spin and spatial exchange signs multiply to −1.

Spin labels ↑ and ↓

Up and down are the two eigenstates of Sz for a spin-½ particle, with eigenvalues +ℏ/2 and −ℏ/2. The order of two arrows gives their slots. The singlet and M = 0 triplet differ in the relative sign between those two orderings. Inspect the spin factors
