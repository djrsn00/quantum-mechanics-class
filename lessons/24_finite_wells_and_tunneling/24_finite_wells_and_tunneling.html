Finite Wells and Tunneling

A detector registers a particle just outside the edge of a finite well. Has containment failed?

Predict what the detector can see. Then compare measurements of a bound state at two times with measurements of a fresh packet sent toward a barrier.

Numerical setup and outgoing estimates

The cell centers are separated by Δx = 0.05 L₀ and cover −95 to 95 L₀. Each barrier setting occupies whole grid cells, and the plot shows their edges. The first and last cells adjoin zero-valued ghost points. Smooth absorbing layers start at |x| = 70 L₀. The ledger adds the measured removal from each layer to the surviving norm.

In scaled units put c = 1/(2mΔx²). The finite-difference Hamiltonian has diagonal 2c + Vj and off-diagonals −c. Each step solves the complex tridiagonal system (I + iΔtH/2)Ψn+1 = (I − iΔtH/2)Ψn with Δt = 0.01 t₀. The initial packet is normalized using the grid sum Σ|Ψj|²Δx. Later steps preserve the surviving scale.

The interaction region includes the barrier and an additional 5 L₀ on each side. Outgoing estimates require time for the incident packet to arrive, low residual interaction mass, and stable outgoing masses. The left and right estimates include probability already removed by their respective layers. The report gives the remaining interaction mass and the status of the separation check.

Mathematical teaching console

Stationary Schrödinger equation

−(ℏ²/2m)ψ″ + V(x)ψ = Eψ

For one energy eigenstate, ψ describes the spatial amplitude. The solution at time t is Ψ(x,t) = ψ(x)e−iEt/ℏ.

Time-dependent Schrödinger equation

iℏ ∂Ψ/∂t = [−(ℏ²/2m) ∂²/∂x² + V(x)]Ψ

A packet generally combines components of different energies. Their different phase rates change the shape of the packet and allow distinct outgoing parts to emerge.

V(x) · Potential

Potential energy at position x. The floor of the well is −V₀ and the top of the barrier is Vb. See the stepped trace in each energy plot.

m · Mass

The mass sets the strength of the kinetic term −ℏ²ψ″/(2m) and the relation K = p²/(2m). Both stations have their own mass control.

E · Energy

An energy eigenvalue. Bound E is measured from the exterior zero and appears in the well ladder. A scattering packet draws from a range of energies.

ψ and Ψ · Amplitude

ψ(x) is a stationary spatial eigenfunction and Ψ(x,t) is a time-dependent state. The amplitude plots show real and imaginary parts. Their squared sum gives position density.

ℏ · Reduced Planck constant

ℏ = h/(2π) links energy to phase rate E/ℏ and momentum to spatial frequency p/ℏ. Our clock labels time in t₀ = ℏ/E₀.

Show Me Why

1. Read the two piecewise potentials

The well has V(x) = −V₀ when |x| < a and V(x) = 0 in the two exterior regions. Its full width is 2a. Bound states have energies between the floor and the exterior zero, −V₀ < E < 0.

The barrier has V(x) = Vb when |x| < w/2 and zero in both leads. Its full width is w. A scattering component incident from a lead can have any positive energy.

2. Get oscillatory and exponential solutions

Rearrange the stationary equation to ψ″ = −[2m(E−V)/ℏ²]ψ. Where E−V is positive the solution is oscillatory. Where E−V is negative the equation gives real exponential solutions.

q = √[2m(E+V₀)]/ℏ inside the well
κ = √(−2mE)/ℏ outside the well

Retain the decaying exterior exponential on each side of the well so the state is normalizable on the full line. The amplitude falls with decay length 1/κ. Density falls as e−2κd at distance d from the edge.

3. Match amplitude and slope at a finite step

With constant mass and a finite step, both ψ and ψ′ are continuous. For an even state use A cos(qx) inside and B e−κ(x−a) to the right. At x = a the two conditions are

A cos(qa) = B
−Aq sin(qa) = −κB
q tan(qa) = κ

On the left, even parity gives the same exterior sign. For an odd state replace cos(qx) by sin(qx). Then A sin(qa) = B and Aq cos(qa) = −κB yield −q cot(qa) = κ. The left exterior has the opposite sign from the right.

4. Find and normalize the allowed bound states

Set z = qa and z₀ = a√(2mV₀)/ℏ, so κa = √(z₀²−z²). Find the root on each physical branch between successive half-integer multiples of π, stopping at z₀. The lab uses an equivalent matching equation without tangent or cotangent poles. It excludes E = 0, where the exterior no longer gives a normalizable tail.

Even: 1/A² = a + sin(2qa)/(2q) + cos²(qa)/κ
Odd: 1/A² = a − sin(2qa)/(2q) + sin²(qa)/κ

The first two terms in each expression give the interior integral before multiplying by A². The last term gives both exterior tails. Recover E = −ℏ²κ²/(2m).

Opening example. With m/m₀ = 1, a/L₀ = 1 and V₀/E₀ = π²/16, the even root is qa = κa = π/4. The energy is −π²E₀/32. Here 1/A² = (1 + 4/π)L₀, of which the two tails contribute (2/π)L₀. The total outside probability is 2/(π+4), about 28.00%, with half on each side.

For an equal-width infinite well the n-th energy above its floor is n²π²ℏ²/[2m(2a)²]. Compare that with E + V₀ for the corresponding finite-well level. Penetration lets the wave extend over a greater distance, which reduces the energy above the floor for that level.

5. Follow transmission through a finite barrier

For an incident component with 0 < E < Vb, the solution in the barrier is C eκbx + D e−κbx, where κb = √[2m(Vb−E)]/ℏ. Matching the value and slope at both edges usually requires both terms. Beyond the barrier the outgoing wave is oscillatory and can carry probability current.

T(E) = [1 + Vb² sinh²(κbw)/(4E(Vb−E))]−1 for 0 < E < Vb

This is a single-energy prediction for equal potentials in both leads. Wider or higher barriers generally reduce transmission under comparable sub-barrier conditions.

For the incident Gaussian, Ψ(x,0) is proportional to e−(x−x₀)²/(4σx²)eip₀(x−x₀)/ℏ. Its momentum spread is Δp = ℏ/(2σx). K₀ = p₀²/(2m) is the central kinetic energy, while ⟨K⟩ = [p₀² + (Δp)²]/(2m) is the mean. Even when K₀ is below Vb, some of the packet can start above the barrier energy. An outgoing packet measures transmission over that distribution.

6. Apply the Born rule to the detectors

P(u ≤ X ≤ v) = ∫uv |Ψ(x,t)|² dx

For a pure bound eigenstate, e−iEt/ℏ cancels in the squared magnitude. A real spatial eigenfunction also gives j = (ℏ/m) Im(Ψ*∂Ψ/∂x) = 0. The outside probability is stationary and no net probability flows through either edge.

Each position draw uses an independent preparation at the same snapshot. The bound detector includes the full line. For the packet, a regional grid sum gives unconditional mass. Dividing that mass by the surviving norm gives the probability among surviving copies. Finite batches fluctuate around these predictions.

Revisit the opening: a bound-state detector can fire outside the well because there is stationary density there. Evidence of transmission comes from a packet’s outgoing far-side component after interaction and separation. Time evolution and probability flow distinguish the two readings.
