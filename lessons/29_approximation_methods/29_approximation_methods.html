Project onto the unperturbed state

Start with a solvable Hamiltonian and add a static perturbation. Expand the energy and ket, then retain terms through first order in λ.

Eₙ = Eₙ⁽⁰⁾ + λEₙ⁽¹⁾ + …
|n⟩ = |n⁽⁰⁾⟩ + λ|n⁽¹⁾⟩ + …
(H₀ − Eₙ⁽⁰⁾)|n⁽¹⁾⟩ = (Eₙ⁽¹⁾ − V)|n⁽⁰⁾⟩

Multiply on the left by ⟨n⁽⁰⁾|. The left side vanishes by the unperturbed eigenvalue equation and Hermiticity. The normalized ket gives Eₙ⁽¹⁾ = ⟨n⁽⁰⁾|V|n⁽⁰⁾⟩. For this oscillator, ⟨n|x⁴|n⟩ = ¾(2n² + 2n + 1) in unperturbed oscillator states.

Projection onto an unperturbed state m ≠ n gives the mixing coefficient λ⟨m⁽⁰⁾|V|n⁽⁰⁾⟩/(Eₙ⁽⁰⁾ − Eₘ⁽⁰⁾). Small gaps can amplify mixing, and degenerate levels require treating the degenerate subspace together. The first-order estimate gains credibility from comparisons over a defined parameter range.

H₀

The solvable starting oscillator, H₀ = −½ d²/dx² + ½x². Its energy labels are Eₙ⁽⁰⁾ = n + ½. Inspect the original potential

λ

The dimensionless coefficient multiplying V once in H = H₀ + λV. Changing λ changes the Hamiltonian for both oscillator workbenches. Adjust the strength

V

The perturbing operator acts by multiplication by x⁴. In the ground Gaussian, the position variance is ½ and ⟨x⁴⟩ = 3(½)². Inspect the distortion

Eₙ⁽¹⁾

The coefficient of λ in the energy expansion equals the expectation of V in the normalized unperturbed state. The first-order change in the energy is λEₙ⁽¹⁾. Inspect the coefficient

Build an upper estimate

In an admissible normalized trial state ψ = Σcₙψₙ, the weights |cₙ|² sum to one. Its energy expectation is Σ|cₙ|²Eₙ ≥ E₀ because every Eₙ ≥ E₀. The upper bound applies to the true ground energy of the selected Hamiltonian. The computed reference has its own numerical error.

Rayleigh quotient

Etrial = ⟨ψ|H|ψ⟩/⟨ψ|ψ⟩. Use a square-integrable trial state with finite energy and the relevant boundary behavior. A nonzero constant on the full line has infinite norm even when a finite plot window makes it look harmless.

⟨K⟩ = b/4
⟨x²/2⟩ = 1/(4b)
λ⟨x⁴⟩ = 3λ/(4b²)
Etrial(b) = b/4 + 1/(4b) + 3λ/(4b²)

At b = 1 the Gaussian equals the unperturbed ground state, so its expectation matches first order. At λ = 0.10, b = 1.20 gives about 0.560417 ℏω. At λ = 0, b = 1 gives the exact value 0.5000 ℏω. Reshape the Gaussian

Follow the local wave scale

In the Gaussian barrier, U = E at x± = ±w√[2 ln(Ub/E)]. The region between those points is forbidden, with allowed leads outside. As the wave number tends to zero near a crossing, the separate leading local amplitude formulas fail. Connection formulas bridge the turning regions.

k

In an allowed region, k(x) = √[2(E−U(x))]. The WKB phase follows ∫k dx and the usual local amplitude is proportional to 1/√k. The ratio |k′|/k² measures how quickly k changes on the wave scale. Move the scanner

κ

In the forbidden region, κ(x) = √[2(U(x)−E)]. The decaying branch has an envelope proportional to exp(−∫κ dx)/√κ away from crossings. Use |κ′|/κ² as a local guide. Inspect the barrier interior

Barrier action A

A = ∫x₋x₊ κ(x) dx. The leading estimate TWKB ≈ exp(−2A) squares the leading amplitude factor and omits a prefactor. Near the top, the turning points approach each other and this estimate can be poor. Compare transmission

Check how much the reference resolves

The oscillator reference solves the full quartic Hamiltonian on a finite grid with zero boundary values. The diagonal is 1/h² + U(xᵢ) and the neighboring entries are −1/(2h²). Sturm counting and bisection locate the low eigenvalues. Grid refinement and domain enlargement probe different sources of numerical error.

The scattering reference integrates ψ″ = 2(U−E)ψ back from an outgoing plane wave in the right lead. Matching the left lead gives incident and reflected amplitudes Ain and Arefl. With equal lead wave numbers and unit transmitted amplitude, T = 1/|Ain|² and R = |Arefl/Ain|². Flux should satisfy R + T ≈ 1. Step and lead checks add evidence beyond that flux test.

Absolute and percentage error

Absolute comparison error is |Qest−Qref| in the observable’s units. Percentage error is 100|Qest−Qref|/|Qref| when the reference is resolved away from zero. Observed refinement changes provide convergence evidence. A tolerance verdict is inconclusive when those changes can alter it. Inspect a saved run
