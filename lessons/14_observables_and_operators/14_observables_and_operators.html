Lesson 14 · Operators and measurement

Observables and Operators

The matrix calculation gives X|0⟩ = |1⟩. If you prepare |0⟩ and measure X, what appears on the instrument?

An operator is a linear map on the state space. A matrix describes that map in a chosen basis. An observable is a measurable quantity represented by a Hermitian operator. In this lesson, the matrix, input column, and product column all use the ordered basis (|0⟩, |1⟩).

Try the algebra and the measurement on the same preparation. The workbench uses the dimensionless Pauli observables X, Y, and Z and ideal measurements in which each possible reading belongs to one eigenket.

What the matrix calculation answers

An operator is linear, so its action on |0⟩ and |1⟩ determines its action on every ket in this two-state space. The first column gives the coordinates of A|0⟩ and the second gives the coordinates of A|1⟩.

A|ψ⟩ = A(α|0⟩ + β|1⟩) = αA|0⟩ + βA|1⟩

A general observable’s mathematical product can change the norm of the input vector. The Pauli matrices preserve it because they are also unitary.

Why Hermitian operators give real readings

In this finite-dimensional setting, an observable is represented by an operator satisfying A = A†. The dagger means take the transpose and conjugate each entry. A Hermitian operator has real eigenvalues and admits an orthonormal eigenbasis.

Take the dagger of Y and check an eigenvalue
  1. Y has rows [0, −i] and [i, 0].
  2. Transposing gives rows [0, i] and [−i, 0].
  3. Conjugating flips the sign of i, restoring rows [0, −i] and [i, 0]. Thus Y† = Y.

In A|a⟩ = a|a⟩, A is the operator, |a⟩ is a nonzero eigenket, and a is the eigenvalue. For a normalized eigenket, ⟨a|A|a⟩ = a⟨a|a⟩ = a. Hermiticity gives (⟨a|A|a⟩)* = ⟨a|A†|a⟩ = ⟨a|A|a⟩. The eigenvalue therefore equals its complex conjugate and is real.

An eigenvalue is a possible reading

Each reading of our ideal nondegenerate measurement corresponds to a single eigenket, up to global phase. If the preparation is that eigenket, its overlap has squared magnitude 1, so the associated reading is definite for that observable. A different observable can use a different eigenbasis.

P(a) = |⟨a|ψ⟩|²

The bra ⟨a| is the conjugate transpose of |a⟩. First take its inner product with the prepared ket. Then square the complex magnitude. After reading a, the measured system is in the associated eigenstate, up to global phase.

Work through Y with the complex preparation

Choose |ψ⟩ = (3|0⟩ + 4i|1⟩)/5. In the matrix product, the first row gives −i(4i/5) = 4/5 and the second gives i(3/5) = 3i/5.

Y|ψ⟩ = (4|0⟩ + 3i|1⟩)/5

For measurement, the +y bra has coefficients (1, −i)/√2. The −y bra has coefficients (1, i)/√2. Conjugation flips the imaginary sign in each ket before taking the inner product.

⟨+y|ψ⟩ = [3/5 + (−i)(4i/5)]/√2 = 7/(5√2)
⟨−y|ψ⟩ = [3/5 + i(4i/5)]/√2 = −1/(5√2)

Squaring magnitudes gives 49/50 for +1 and 1/50 for −1. For the same preparation, measuring X gives 1/2 and 1/2, while measuring Z gives 9/25 and 16/25, in +1, −1 order.
