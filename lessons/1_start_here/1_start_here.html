The math and a worked 30° example

Project the prepared direction onto the two analyzer axes and square the components. With preparation θ and analyzer angle a, the probabilities are cos²(θ − a) along the first axis and sin²(θ − a) along the perpendicular axis. Both angles are measured from horizontal.

With θ = 30° and a = 0°, the horizontal component is √3/2 and the vertical component is 1/2. Squaring gives P(H) = 3/4 and P(V) = 1/4. The two probabilities sum to 1. The same intensity ratios occur in classical optics. These statistics alone cannot establish uniquely quantum behavior.

Work out the transmission

The first ideal filter passes half of an unpolarized beam on average. Each transmitted photon leaves polarized along the filter axis. At the next active filter, multiply by cos² of the angle between the two active axes.

All emitted photons × 1/2 × cos²(45° − 0°) × cos²(90° − 45°) = 1/8 transmitted in the model. That is 1/4 of those passed by the first filter.

How routing prepares the next state

The selected output prepares polarization along its own axis. Selecting the perpendicular output prepares the orthogonal state. Multiply the probability of reaching the last analyzer by its conditional output probability to get the share of all sent photons.

How the waves add

For position x and time t, let u = 2πx/λ − ωt, where λ is the wavelength and ω is the angular frequency. Add A cos u and B cos(u + φ) point by point. With relative phase φ, the resultant amplitude is √(A² + B² + 2AB cos φ).

Why marking changes the counts

With the bright output at zero phase labeled D0, P(D0) = (1 + m cos φ)/2 and P(D1) = 1 − P(D0). The marker becomes correlated with the path. Ignoring the marker reduces the interference term in the detector counts as the overlap falls. An output click alone supplies no observed intermediate path.

Optional mission · Maximize fringe visibility

Adjust m and scan the phase. Visibility is (maximum − minimum)/(maximum + minimum) for the D0 probability over a full cycle. One phase reading alone cannot determine it.

Amplitudes, phase, and optical transformations

Write |ψ⟩ = α|H⟩ + β|V⟩ in component order (H, V). Here α = cos γ and β = eiδ sin γ. The mixing angle γ sets the balance. δ is the phase of V relative to H. Normalization means |α|² + |β|² = 1.

Light propagates toward the viewer. Looking toward the source, H points right, V points up, and positive axis angles turn from H toward V. The field-tip view is a classical electric-field illustration of Re(|ψ⟩e−iωt). Detector clicks are individual photon measurements.

The polarizer projects onto its linear axis. The squared norm after projection is the survival probability. If it is positive, divide the projected state by its norm. If it is zero, there is no transmitted state. In each plate’s local axes, the fast component keeps its phase and the perpendicular component gains π for the half-wave plate and π/2 for the quarter-wave plate.

For each analyzer output, square the magnitude of the inner product with the incoming normalized state. The diagonal vectors are (H ± V)/√2. The circular labels C+ and C− mean (H + iV)/√2 and (H − iV)/√2, respectively.
