Try changing a sign

In Product mode, set A and B to |+⟩. Change just B to |−⟩ and inspect the two rows with B = 1. Their amplitudes change sign, but squaring their magnitudes gives the same computational-basis probabilities. A measurement in the |+⟩, |−⟩ basis can distinguish the two B preparations.

Build the joint basis

Each qubit has two computational basis states, |0⟩ and |1⟩. The tensor product, written ⊗, pairs each basis state of A with each basis state of B. Write i for A’s bit and j for B’s bit, with each bit taking the value 0 or 1.

|ij⟩ = |i⟩A ⊗ |j⟩B

Ordered basis (|00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩, |11⟩)

dim(ℋA ⊗ ℋB) = 2 × 2 = 4

ℋA and ℋB are the single-qubit state spaces. Their dimensions multiply because each of A’s two basis choices has two partners from B. Measuring both qubits in this basis gives one ordered result per trial. For instance, the outcome (0, 1) records A = 0 and B = 1 together.

Expand a product state

Let |u⟩A and |v⟩B be normalized single-qubit states. The letters a, b, c, and d are their amplitudes. An amplitude may be complex, and |α|² denotes the squared magnitude of an amplitude α.

|u⟩A = a|0⟩ + b|1⟩

|v⟩B = c|0⟩ + d|1⟩

|a|² + |b|² = 1,   |c|² + |d|² = 1

The tensor product distributes over addition in each factor. Pair the summands, multiply their amplitudes, and place each product beside its joint basis ket.

|u⟩A ⊗ |v⟩B = (a|0⟩ + b|1⟩) ⊗ (c|0⟩ + d|1⟩)

= ac|00⟩ + ad|01⟩ + bc|10⟩ + bd|11⟩

The ordered amplitude vector is (ac, ad, bc, bd). For example, the coefficient of |01⟩ is ad because it pairs A’s |0⟩ amplitude a with B’s |1⟩ amplitude d.

Read joint and marginal probabilities

For a normalized pure state, let αij be the amplitude of |ij⟩ in the joint basis. The Born rule gives the probability of seeing both indicated results on the same trial.

P(A = i, B = j) = |αij|²

A marginal probability answers a question about just one of the qubits. To find it, add the probabilities of the mutually exclusive pairs that have the required bit. Here P(01) abbreviates the joint probability P(A = 0, B = 1).

P(A = 0) = P(00) + P(01)

P(B = 0) = P(00) + P(10)

For the worked preparation |+⟩A ⊗ |1⟩B, P(A = 0) = 0 + 1/2 = 1/2, and P(B = 0) = 0 + 0 = 0. For outcome 1, sum the two records with a 1 in the appropriate position.

Same marginals, different joint records

Preparing |+⟩ on both qubits gives four amplitudes of 1/2. The Bell preparation |Φ⁺⟩ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2 places all its amplitude on the two matching pairs.

Each preparation gives 50/50 marginals for A and for B. Keep the ordered pairs across repeated trials and check how often the two bits disagree. The product preparation disagrees half the time in theory. The ideal Bell preparation always agrees. A single 01 rules out that ideal Bell preparation, while a single 00 is compatible with both.

Why the Bell ket cannot factor

Suppose the Bell ket were a product. Then its |00⟩ and |11⟩ coefficients would be ac and bd, both nonzero. That forces all four factors a, b, c, and d to be nonzero. It follows that ad and bc must also be nonzero, contradicting the zeros on |01⟩ and |10⟩.

The argument establishes entanglement for the specified pure Bell ket. The same joint frequencies in this basis also arise from a classical 50/50 mixture of |00⟩ and |11⟩, so these frequencies alone cannot certify entanglement. Show the math

Read the factors off ratios. If a ≠ 0, the A = 0 row (ac, ad) is a times (c, d), so B’s factor is proportional to the first row. If b ≠ 0, the second row (bc, bd) is b times the same (c, d). For a product, the two rows of the 2×2 grid must be proportional.

A quick recipe: pick a nonzero row as (c, d), then a and b are the numbers that scale it into each row. Normalizing each factor is optional; the product test α00α11 − α01α10 = 0 is the condition that the rows are proportional.

Product or Entangled?

A pure pair state is a product exactly when D = α00α11 − α01α10 = 0. The grid shows the two diagonals: the solid one multiplies α00α11, the dashed one α01α10. Equal diagonal products mean the rows are proportional, so the state factors; any difference, however small, means it is entangled. Pick a card and compute D yourself, or tick the box to see it.

Show the math

For a product, α00α11 − α01α10 = (ac)(bd) − (ad)(bc) = 0. Conversely, if D = 0 the two rows (α00, α01) and (α10, α11) are proportional, which is exactly the condition for factors to exist.

D is the determinant of the 2×2 grid of amplitudes. Its size |D| runs from 0 for products to 1/2 for the Bell states. Disguised products hide their factors behind signs: (|00⟩ − |01⟩ + |10⟩ − |11⟩)/2 = |+⟩A ⊗ |−⟩B.

Conditional Odds

A conditional probability answers: given that A gave i, how likely is each result for B? Keep only the pairs with A = i, then rescale them to add to 1: P(B = j | A = i) = P(A = i, B = j)/P(A = i). Show the math

P(B = j | A = i) = P(ij)/P(A = i), defined when P(A = i) > 0. Conditioning the other way, P(A = i | B = j) = P(ij)/P(B = j), uses a column instead of a row.

For a product state P(ij) = P(A = i)P(B = j), so P(B = j | A = i) = P(B = j): knowing A tells you nothing about B. The converse fails: equal conditional odds in this one basis don’t prove a product, because phases such as the i in (|00⟩ + |01⟩ + |10⟩ + i|11⟩)/2 never show up in computational-basis probabilities.

    Entangling Gate (CNOT)

    This is a gentle preview of quantum circuits. Time runs left to right: qubit A on the top wire, B below. CNOT uses A as the control and B as the target: it flips B when A is 1 and does nothing when A is 0, so |10⟩ ↔ |11⟩. Unlike gates on one qubit at a time, it can turn a product input into an entangled output. Enter the input factors and read D for the input and the output.

    When CNOT doesn’t entangle✓ Complete

    Find normalized inputs with both a ≠ 0 and b ≠ 0 for which the CNOT output is still a product state (D = 0).

    Show the math

    CNOT sends (α00, α01, α10, α11) to (α00, α01, α11, α10). On a product input (a|0⟩ + b|1⟩) ⊗ (c|0⟩ + d|1⟩) the output is (ac, ad, bd, bc), with D = ac·bc − ad·bd = ab(c² − d²).

    So the output is entangled unless a = 0, b = 0 or c² = d²; the last holds for B in (|0⟩ ± |1⟩)/√2, which X leaves alone up to a sign. |+⟩A ⊗ |0⟩B gives (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2 = |Φ⁺⟩.

    Dimension Counter

    Each qubit you add doubles the number of joint basis states: n qubits have 2ⁿ of them, from |00…0⟩ to |11…1⟩, and a general state needs a complex amplitude for each. n classical bits also have 2ⁿ configurations, but they sit in just one of them at a time, written down with n bits. Slide n and compare the counts, or tick the box to pair a qubit with a three-level system.

    Products are a small corner✓ Complete

    For n = 5, a product of five single-qubit states is fixed by five pairs of amplitudes. How many amplitudes is that, and how many does a general 5-qubit state need?

    Show the math

    Dimensions multiply: dim(ℋ₁ ⊗ ℋ₂) = dim ℋ₁ × dim ℋ₂. For n qubits, 2 × 2 × … × 2 = 2ⁿ; for a qubit and a three-level system, 2 × 3 = 6.

    A product of n qubit states is fixed by 2n amplitudes, two per factor, while a general state needs 2ⁿ. Already at n = 5 that is 10 against 32, so most states of many qubits are entangled. Normalization and the global phase remove one complex number’s worth of freedom from each count.

    Build a state with all four joint probabilities equal to 1/4 that is clearly entangled: |D| ≥ 0.1.

    Build a state that never gives 11, with P(A = 0) = 1/2 and P(B = 0) = 2/3.

    Card 5 · can it be built?✓ Complete

    The card asks for a product state with 50/50 marginals for both qubits whose bits match 80% of the time.

    Show the math

    Probabilities fix only magnitudes: pick |αij| = √P(ij), then choose phases freely, unless the card also asks for D. For 50/50 margins with an 80% match, P(00) = P(11) = 0.4 and P(01) = P(10) = 0.1.

    A product has P(ij) = P(A = i)P(B = j). With 50/50 margins every P(ij) = 1/4, so P(match) = 1/2. With all P(ij) = 1/4, D = (1/4)(e^(i(φ00 + φ11)) − e^(i(φ01 + φ10))), so one minus sign gives |D| = 1/2.

    Error Detective

    Each case file holds a worked solution with exactly one planted mistake. Find the first line that goes wrong, and name the mistake. The diagram shows what the solution claims next to what is actually true.

    Error Detective controls

    Case 1 · expanding a product✓ Complete

    Expand ((3/5)|0⟩ + (4/5)|1⟩)A ⊗ ((1/√2)|0⟩ + (1/√2)|1⟩)B and find P(01).

    1. The |01⟩ term pairs A’s |0⟩ amplitude 3/5 with B’s |1⟩ amplitude 1/√2.
    2. So α01 = 3/5 + 1/√2 ≈ 1.307.
    3. P(01) = |α01|² ≈ 1.709.
    4. So 01 is the most likely record.
    Case 2 · which bit comes first✓ Complete

    Prepare |1⟩A ⊗ ((3/5)|0⟩ + (4/5)|1⟩)B. Find P(A = 0).

    1. A is certainly 1; B gives 0 with probability 9/25.
    2. The joint state is (3/5)|01⟩ + (4/5)|11⟩.
    3. P(A = 0) = P(00) + P(01) = 0 + 9/25.
    4. So A gives 0 on about a third of the trials.
    Case 3 · a negative amplitude✓ Complete

    Find the joint probabilities for (1/2)|00⟩ + (1/2)|01⟩ − (1/√2)|11⟩.

    1. The amplitudes are α00 = 1/2, α01 = 1/2, α10 = 0 and α11 = −1/√2.
    2. P(00) = P(01) = 1/4 and P(10) = 0.
    3. P(11) = −1/2, because its amplitude is negative.
    4. The total is 1/4 + 1/4 + 0 − 1/2 = 0, so the state is not normalized.
    Case 4 · a joint probability✓ Complete

    For √0.3|00⟩ + √0.7|11⟩, find P(00).

    1. P(A = 0) = |α00|² + |α01|² = 0.3.
    2. P(B = 0) = |α00|² + |α10|² = 0.3.
    3. Both qubits are measured, so P(00) = P(A = 0) × P(B = 0) = 0.09.
    4. So the bits disagree with probability 1 − 0.09 − 0.49 = 0.42.
    Case 5 · what the records show✓ Complete

    A source gives 1000 computational-basis records: 503 of 00, 497 of 11 and no 01 or 10.

    1. The bits matched on 1000 of 1000 trials.
    2. P(A = 0) ≈ 0.503 and P(B = 0) ≈ 0.503, both close to 1/2.
    3. No pure product state gives perfectly matched bits with 50/50 marginals.
    4. So the source must be sending the entangled state |Φ⁺⟩.
    Case 6 · four equal magnitudes✓ Complete

    Is (|00⟩ − |01⟩ − |10⟩ − |11⟩)/2 a product state?

    1. Every amplitude has magnitude 1/2, so every P(ij) = 1/4.
    2. Both marginals are 50/50.
    3. The minus signs change no probability, so this is the same state as (|00⟩ + |01⟩ + |10⟩ + |11⟩)/2 = |+⟩|+⟩.
    4. So it is a product state, with D = 0.
    Show the math

    The planted mistakes: joint amplitudes multiply, αij = (A’s amplitude for i) × (B’s amplitude for j). A comes first: |ij⟩ has A’s bit i. A probability is a squared magnitude, never negative. P(ij) = P(A = i)P(B = j) only for a product state.

    Perfectly matched computational-basis records are consistent with |Φ⁺⟩, but a classical 50/50 mixture of |00⟩ and |11⟩ gives the same records. A relative sign can turn a product into an entangled state even when no single probability changes.

    Builds on Lesson 17

    Interaction Builds Entanglement

    Lesson 17’s Two Spins preview kept two spins independent, each in its own field. Now couple them with the interaction H = J Z⊗Z. Each joint basis ket is an energy eigenstate: Z⊗Z|ij⟩ = +|ij⟩ when the bits match and −|ij⟩ when they differ, so α_ij(t) = α_ij(0) e^(∓iJt/ℏ). The matched and mismatched terms drift apart in phase, and a product start becomes entangled.

    Watch D = α00α11 − α01α10: it starts at 0 and rises as the pair entangles. Play respects your reduced-motion setting and the Reduced effects box; Step always works.

    Units: J is an energy, measured for example in eV, and J/ℏ is an angular frequency in rad/s. The box takes J/ℏ, which may include π.

    Builds on Lesson 17: the time evolution U(t) = exp(−iHt/ℏ), now for a Hamiltonian that acts on both qubits at once.

    An unbalanced start✓ Complete

    Opening this mission starts the pair in ((√3/2)|0⟩ + (1/2)|1⟩)A ⊗ |+⟩B. What is the largest |D| it ever reaches?

    Show the math

    For a product start (a|0⟩ + b|1⟩) ⊗ (c|0⟩ + d|1⟩), α(t) = (ac e^(−iθ), ad e^(iθ), bc e^(iθ), bd e^(−iθ)) with θ = Jt/ℏ, so D(t) = abcd (e^(−2iθ) − e^(2iθ)) = −2i abcd sin 2θ.

    For |+⟩|+⟩, abcd = 1/4 and D = −(i/2) sin 2θ: |D| peaks at θ = π/4 and returns to 0 at θ = π/2, where the state is −i|−⟩|−⟩. If a, b, c or d is 0, D stays 0 forever.
