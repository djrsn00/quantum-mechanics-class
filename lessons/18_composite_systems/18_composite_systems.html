Try changing a sign

In Product mode, set A and B to |+⟩. Change just B to |−⟩ and inspect the two rows with B = 1. Their amplitudes change sign, but squaring their magnitudes gives the same computational-basis probabilities. A measurement in the |+⟩, |−⟩ basis can distinguish the two B preparations.

Build the joint basis

Each qubit has two computational basis states, |0⟩ and |1⟩. The tensor product, written ⊗, pairs each basis state of A with each basis state of B. Write i for A’s bit and j for B’s bit, with each bit taking the value 0 or 1.

|ij⟩ = |i⟩A ⊗ |j⟩B

Ordered basis (|00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩, |11⟩)

dim(ℋA ⊗ ℋB) = 2 × 2 = 4

ℋA and ℋB are the single-qubit state spaces. Their dimensions multiply because each of A’s two basis choices has two partners from B. Measuring both qubits in this basis gives one ordered result per trial. For instance, the outcome (0, 1) records A = 0 and B = 1 together.

Expand a product state

Let |u⟩A and |v⟩B be normalized single-qubit states. The letters a, b, c, and d are their amplitudes. An amplitude may be complex, and |α|² denotes the squared magnitude of an amplitude α.

|u⟩A = a|0⟩ + b|1⟩

|v⟩B = c|0⟩ + d|1⟩

|a|² + |b|² = 1,   |c|² + |d|² = 1

The tensor product distributes over addition in each factor. Pair the summands, multiply their amplitudes, and place each product beside its joint basis ket.

|u⟩A ⊗ |v⟩B = (a|0⟩ + b|1⟩) ⊗ (c|0⟩ + d|1⟩)

= ac|00⟩ + ad|01⟩ + bc|10⟩ + bd|11⟩

The ordered amplitude vector is (ac, ad, bc, bd). For example, the coefficient of |01⟩ is ad because it pairs A’s |0⟩ amplitude a with B’s |1⟩ amplitude d.

Worked example · A in |+⟩ and B in |1⟩

For A, both amplitudes equal 1/√2. For B, the amplitudes of |0⟩ and |1⟩ are 0 and 1.

|+⟩A ⊗ |1⟩B

= (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2 ⊗ (0|0⟩ + 1|1⟩)

= (|01⟩ + |11⟩)/√2

Read joint and marginal probabilities

For a normalized pure state, let αij be the amplitude of |ij⟩ in the joint basis. The Born rule gives the probability of seeing both indicated results on the same trial.

P(A = i, B = j) = |αij|²

A marginal probability answers a question about just one of the qubits. To find it, add the probabilities of the mutually exclusive pairs that have the required bit. Here P(01) abbreviates the joint probability P(A = 0, B = 1).

P(A = 0) = P(00) + P(01)

P(B = 0) = P(00) + P(10)

For the worked preparation |+⟩A ⊗ |1⟩B, P(A = 0) = 0 + 1/2 = 1/2, and P(B = 0) = 0 + 0 = 0. For outcome 1, sum the two records with a 1 in the appropriate position.

Same marginals, different joint records

Preparing |+⟩ on both qubits gives four amplitudes of 1/2. The Bell preparation |Φ⁺⟩ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2 places all its amplitude on the two matching pairs.

Each preparation gives 50/50 marginals for A and for B. Keep the ordered pairs across repeated trials and check how often the two bits disagree. The product preparation disagrees half the time in theory. The ideal Bell preparation always agrees. A single 01 rules out that ideal Bell preparation, while a single 00 is compatible with both.

Why the Bell ket cannot factor

Suppose the Bell ket were a product. Then its |00⟩ and |11⟩ coefficients would be ac and bd, both nonzero. That forces all four factors a, b, c, and d to be nonzero. It follows that ad and bc must also be nonzero, contradicting the zeros on |01⟩ and |10⟩.

The argument establishes entanglement for the specified pure Bell ket. The same joint frequencies in this basis also arise from a classical 50/50 mixture of |00⟩ and |11⟩, so these frequencies alone cannot certify entanglement.
