Can two systems look the same right now and still do different things next?

What information makes a state?

The system is the thing we choose to describe. Its state gives the changing information needed to make predictions under a particular model. The model tells us which quantities are fixed and what rule to apply. The evolution rule tells you how the state changes with time or after an action.

Consider a switch with two possible settings, off and on. The rule is fixed: one toggle flips the current setting. To predict the result of one toggle, you only need one bit: 0 for off or 1 for on.

A cart needs a different description. Its position can vary continuously, and a picture of that position gives you no direct reading of how fast it is moving or in which direction.

Cart lab

Each cart has constant mass and moves along a straight track. The net force along the track is zero, so each cart keeps its signed velocity. The tracks are independent. Right is positive, left is negative, and zero velocity means the cart stays put.

A snapshot is one observation

An observation is a piece of information you record. In this lab, the initial position is one observation. Several different velocities fit that same snapshot, so it leaves the future position uncertain to you, even though the model has a definite future for each complete state.

Keep velocity hidden, advance one second, and compare the starting position with the new position. Which additional observation lets you infer the velocity?

Check your reasoning

Take another position reading of the same cart at a known later time. Subtract the first position from the second, then divide by the elapsed time. In this constant velocity model, that gives the signed velocity. A negative result means leftward motion.

For example, a cart at 0 m at 0 s and −2 m at 1 s has velocity −2 m/s. Both readings and the time between them matter.

Show the math for constant velocity

The initial state for this model is the pair (x0, v0). The evolution rule comes from the constant velocity assumption. At a time t greater than zero, velocity equals signed displacement divided by the time between readings, Δt. When the first reading is at the start, Δt = t.

v0 = Δx / Δt

Δx = v0t

x(t) = x0 + v0t

Multiplying by t gives the displacement. Adding that signed displacement to the initial position gives the final position. The final equation also gives the correct starting point at 0 s.

x0
Initial position measured from the origin, in metres (m).
v0
Initial signed velocity, in metres per second (m/s). It stays constant in this model.
t
Time since the start of the trial, in seconds (s).
Δx and x(t)
Displacement from the starting point and position measured from the origin, respectively. Both are in metres.

Reproduce this run

Select cart A only. Set the starting position to +1 m, show velocity, and set it to −2 m/s. Advance twice. The displacement after 2 s is (−2 m/s) × (2 s) = −4 m. The final position is +1 m + (−4 m) = −3 m.

Initial values describe where a trial starts. The rule describes how it changes. If we added friction, the same initial position and velocity could produce a different later position.

What would a quantum state tell us?

Under their stated rules, the switch and cart are deterministic. Specifying the state fixes the prediction for a given time or action. Classical models can also include randomness, such as a cart disturbed by random kicks.

Hiding velocity illustrates missing information in this classical model. The exercise leaves open how to explain quantum probabilities. As you move into waves and interference, keep asking what information a quantum state supplies and how it connects to what a measurement can show.

Math and missions

For zero net force, x = x₀ + v₀t and v = v₀. For constant acceleration a, x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at² and v = v₀ + at. Negative accelerations point left.

For friction with slowing rate f > 0, let u = min(t, |v₀|/f). Then x = x₀ + v₀u − ½ sign(v₀)fu² and v = sign(v₀) max(|v₀| − ft, 0). This level-track model leaves the cart at rest after it stops. With f = 0 it coasts.
