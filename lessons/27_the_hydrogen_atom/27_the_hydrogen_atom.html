Show Me Why

Rₙℓ · Radial function

The distance-dependent amplitude. It obeys ∫₀∞ |Rₙℓ|²r²dr = 1. The scanner shows its squared magnitude times r².

Yℓᵐ · Angular function

The direction-dependent amplitude, with ∫ |Yℓᵐ|²dΩ = 1. It controls the lobes and angular zeros in the slices.

n · Principal level

Positive integer labeling the energy Eₙ = −EH/n² and setting the range of ℓ. The lab supports n = 1, 2, 3.

ℓ · Orbital index

Integer from 0 to n−1. It fixes L² = ℏ²ℓ(ℓ+1) and the spectroscopic letter s, p, or d in this lab.

m · Projection index

Integer from −ℓ to +ℓ. It fixes Lz = ℏm and gives the eimφ azimuthal phase factor.

|ψ|² · Spatial density

Squared magnitude of the spatial amplitude in aH⁻³. Multiply by an infinitesimal volume to obtain probability.

aH · Hydrogen length scale

aH = 4πε₀ℏ²/(μe²) uses the electron–proton reduced mass. The probe, shell, and plot use this length as their unit.

Eₙ · Bound energy

The energy relative to ionization, in electronvolts. The bay subtracts the two selected levels.

1. Separate center-of-mass motion and find the Coulomb bound states

With no external field, center-of-mass motion is free. For the relative coordinate, the reduced mass is μ = mₑmₚ/(mₑ+mₚ). The potential depends only on the electron–proton distance r, so spherical coordinates separate the radial and angular equations.

V(r) = −e²/(4πε₀r)
Ĥ = −(ℏ²/2μ)∇² + V(r)
aH = 4πε₀ℏ²/(μe²) = (mₑ/μ)a₀

The symbol a₀ denotes the fixed-nucleus Bohr radius. This lab consistently uses aH. Write ψₙℓₘ = Rₙℓ(r)Yℓᵐ(θ,φ). Here θ measures from +z and φ measures around z. The angular eigenvalue ℏ²ℓ(ℓ+1) of L² supplies the radial centrifugal term. With χ = rR, the radial equation is

−(ℏ²/2μ) χ″ + [ℏ²ℓ(ℓ+1)/(2μr²) − e²/(4πε₀r)]χ = Eχ

Regularity at the origin selects R ∼ rℓ. A negative energy gives a decaying exponential at large r. Factoring out both behaviors leaves an associated Laguerre equation. A normalizable bound solution terminates at polynomial degree n−ℓ−1, giving the allowed n values and energies.

u = 2r/(naH)
Rₙℓ = (2/(naH))3/2 √[(n−ℓ−1)!/(2n(n+ℓ)!)] e−u/2 uℓ Ln−ℓ−12ℓ+1(u)
Eₙ = −μe⁴/[2(4πε₀)²ℏ²n²] ≈ −13.6 eV/n²

Zero energy is the ionization limit. This nonrelativistic spatial model omits spin-dependent structure, relativistic corrections, fine structure, and hyperfine structure.

2. Connect quantum numbers, degeneracy, and nodes

For each n ≥ 1, choose ℓ = 0 through n−1, then any integer m from −ℓ to +ℓ. This gives 1 + 4 + 9 = 14 spatial eigenstates through n = 3. The same angular functions appeared in Angular Momentum, with L² = ℏ²ℓ(ℓ+1) and Lz = ℏm. The radial function supplies the distance dependence.

There are n−ℓ−1 positive-radius radial nodes. In 2s the radial factor is proportional to (2−r/aH)e−r/(2aH), so its node is at 2aH. For 2p, m = 0 the angular factor is proportional to cos θ, giving an equatorial nodal plane. For 2p, m = ±1 the angular factor is proportional to sin θ e±iφ, up to a constant phase. The density vanishes on the z-axis and is independent of azimuth.

Opposite m values have the same density and opposite phase winding. Real pₓ and pᵧ angular functions are linear combinations of m = ±1. In the lab’s definite-m basis, phase at a point includes the sign of R. It is undefined wherever ψ vanishes.

The ideal Coulomb energy depends on n alone. The 2s and 2p states therefore share an energy, with different angular momentum and different position distributions.

3. Work the opening experiment from the volume element

dP = |ψ|² r² sin θ dr dθ dφ
Pᵣ(r) = r² |Rₙℓ(r)|², because ∫ |Yℓᵐ|²dΩ = 1
P(a ≤ r ≤ b) = ∫ab Pᵣ(r) dr

The point probe reports density per unit volume. The radial scanner collects all directions in each spherical shell and reports probability per unit radius. The r² factor accounts for the growing area of a shell. A tiny volume around the origin can have high density and still contain little probability.

1s: |ψ₁₀₀|² = e−2x/(πaH³)
Pᵣ(r) = 4x²e−2x/aH, with x = r/aH

The point density is maximal at r = 0. The derivative of radial density with respect to x is (8/aH) x(1−x)e−2x. It changes from positive to negative at x = 1, so a narrow-bin distance histogram tends to peak near aH. The cumulative probability at x = 1 is 1−5e−2 ≈ 32.33%. That is about 32 detections in a batch of 100 on average, with finite-sample variation.

The mean is ⟨r⟩ = (aH/2)[3n²−ℓ(ℓ+1)], or 1.5aH for 1s. This integral averages across the whole distribution. Local radial peaks can occur on either side of a node, and none is required to equal the mean.

4. Check a photon gap and an electric-dipole channel

ΔE = Ef−Ei
Eγ = |ΔE| = hν = hc/λ for a nonzero gap

Positive ΔE requires absorption. Negative ΔE permits an emission energy. A zero gap supplies no finite-wavelength line in this model. To check a single-photon electric-dipole channel, the operator is proportional to a component of r. Its angular part has rank one and odd parity, giving Δℓ = ±1 and Δm = 0, ±1. Polarization relative to the z axis determines which Δm channels can contribute.

For 2p, m = 0 → 1s, |ΔE| is about 10.20 eV and the vacuum wavelength is about 121.57 nm in the ultraviolet. The 2s → 1s gap agrees, but Δℓ = 0 fails this single-photon electric-dipole rule. Other processes, such as two-photon decay, lie outside this check.

Revisit the opening. The volume factor is why the spatial density can peak at the proton while distances cluster around a shell farther away.
