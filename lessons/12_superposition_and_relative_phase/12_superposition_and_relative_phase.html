Lesson 12 · Phase and interference

Superposition and Relative Phase

Two preparations each give 50/50 predictions when measured in the 0/1 basis. Can another measurement distinguish them with certainty?

Start with the normalized state |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩. The symbol ψ names the state, and α and β are the complex amplitudes for the two basis kets. A superposition is a linear combination of basis states. Which components appear depends on the basis you choose.

|ψ(φ, γ)⟩ = (eiγ/√2)(|0⟩ + eiφ|1⟩)

In the lab, φ is the phase of β relative to α, and γ is the common phase. Here i² = −1, and eiθ turns a complex number through angle θ without changing its magnitude. Both amplitudes have magnitude 1/√2, so their squared magnitudes always add to 1.

The +/− measurement

These panels compare two alternative measurements on identically prepared systems. The arrows represent complex amplitudes. Squaring their lengths in the selected basis gives the predicted probabilities.

|+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2 · |−⟩ = (|0⟩ − |1⟩)/√2

The ket |+⟩ has two components in the 0/1 basis. It is itself one of the basis kets in the +/− basis.

Why the measurement basis matters

In the 0/1 basis, each outcome depends on the magnitude of a single coefficient. In the +/− basis, each outcome depends on a combination of the two coefficients. The phase between the contributions can make them reinforce or cancel.

Work through the amplitudes and probabilities

The basis kets are |+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2 and |−⟩ = (|0⟩ − |1⟩)/√2. Taking their inner products with |ψ⟩ gives the amplitudes in that basis. The cross inner products ⟨0|1⟩ and ⟨1|0⟩ vanish.

c± = (α ± β)/√2 = eiγ(1 ± eiφ)/2

Combine the complex contributions first. To find the probability, square the magnitude of their sum or difference. Since |eiγ| = 1, the common phase drops out.

|1 ± eiφ|² = (1 ± eiφ)(1 ± e−iφ) = 2 ± 2 cos φ

P(+) = (1 + cos φ)/2 = cos²(φ/2)
P(−) = (1 − cos φ)/2 = sin²(φ/2)

A zero amplitude has no defined phase. At γ = 0, the states at φ = 0 and φ = π are |+⟩ and |−⟩. Both give equal 0/1 predictions, yet the +/− measurement distinguishes those two preparations with certainty.

Rotate both arrows together

Choose a relative phase and hold it fixed. Move the global-phase slider and watch both complex coefficients rotate together. The angle between them and all probability readouts stay fixed.

|eiγc|² = |eiγ|² |c|² = |c|²

Here c can be any measurement-basis amplitude. A global phase has no observable effect for this isolated state. A relative phase can change predictions in a suitable basis. Equal predictions in one basis alone are insufficient to identify the physical state.

Coherent superposition and random preparation

Compare the coherent preparation |+⟩ with a procedure that randomly prepares |0⟩ or |1⟩ with equal chances. The procedures agree on the 0/1 predictions.

PreparationP(0), P(1)P(+), P(−)
Coherent |+⟩1/2, 1/21, 0
50/50 random |0⟩ or |1⟩1/2, 1/21/2, 1/2

In the random procedure, each preparation gives equal +/− probabilities, and averaging those probabilities keeps them equal. In the coherent state the amplitudes combine first. The fixed relative phase makes their contributions cancel for the minus outcome.
